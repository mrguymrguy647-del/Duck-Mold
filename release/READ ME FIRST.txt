DUCK MOLD
Make 3D models for Duck Forge games from parts that join at sockets,
with the mouse or from a text recipe.


TO START
  Double-click DuckMoldStudio.exe.

  The first time, Windows may say "Windows protected your PC". That's
  because the program isn't signed with a (paid) certificate yet, not
  because something is wrong. Click "More info", then "Run anyway".
  Windows only asks once.

  For a desktop icon, double-click "Create desktop shortcut.bat".
  Keep this folder where it is afterwards: the shortcut points into it.


A FIRST LOOK
  The Studio opens on its start page. Click one of the examples (the
  robot, say): a copy of your own opens, in the folder "Duck Mold" in
  your Documents, so you can change it as you like. The first time,
  five small tips show you round (Help > Show the tips again brings
  them back).
  - Drag to turn the model, right-drag to slide it, roll the wheel to
    come closer.
  - Click a part to select it. Its properties are on the right: change
    its size or colour there.
  - "Add", at the top, puts a new shape on the ground, joined to
    nothing. Drag it by its arrows, or drag the shape itself.
  - The buttons at the top left of the picture are the tools: Move
    (arrows), Rotate (rings), Scale (boxes) and Size (dots on the
    faces: pull one to make the shape longer or wider). Their keys are
    G, R, S and T. Hold Ctrl while dragging to move without snapping.
  - Click one of the small round markers on the selected part and pick
    a shape: a new part is joined there.
  - Drag the selected part onto another part's marker to join it there.
    "Unjoin", on the right, makes a joined part free again.
  - Select a box and press Tab to shape it: click a face (its top,
    say) and drag its arrow to pull it out, or push it in. I gives a
    knob instead: drag it in to make a smaller face inside, with a
    frame round it (a window). The panel lists every change, to change
    a number or take one away. Tab goes back to parts. E shows the
    edges and C the corners, to move one; K cuts round the box, for
    more faces to shape.
  - Shift+click selects several parts, to move or colour together.
    Right-click a part for its menu. Delete removes it; Ctrl+D copies
    it; H hides it so you can get at what is behind (Shift+H shows it
    again).
  - "Front", "Side", "Top", "3/4" and "Back" turn the view. "Wire" lets
    you see through the model.
  - Facing you, the model's left is on your right, as a person's is: a
    pose that looks to its left turns its head to the right of the
    screen. The words "its left" and "its right" on the ground say which
    side is which, and "Back" shows it from behind, the way round you
    expect.
  - "Export" writes the model as a .glb file next to the recipe, for a
    Duck Forge game. "Render picture" writes a picture of it.
  - Undo is Ctrl+Z. Help > The keys lists every key.

  "New model" starts a recipe of your own with one box in it.

  You can also drop a recipe file on DuckMoldStudio.exe to open it.


PICTURES ON MODELS  (new in 1.0)
  A part, or one face of it, can show a picture: a face, a label, wood
  planks. Open the robot, Pip or the plank crate from the start page to
  see.
  - Select a part. Under "Images" on the right, "New image..." makes a
    picture the right shape for the part or for one of its faces.
  - "Paint in Duck Quill" opens the picture in Duck Quill (another free
    Duck program), with thin lines that show the face. Paint, press
    Ctrl+S there, and the model here has it. Without Duck Quill the
    picture opens in whatever you use for pictures: it is a plain PNG
    file in the folder "textures" beside your model.
  - The picture lies over the part's colour. Where you paint nothing,
    the colour shows.
  - Under the palette is a row of the model's pictures. Click one, then
    a face, to put it there. The first square takes a picture off.

  FOR A GAME
  - With nothing selected, the panel on the right says where "Export"
    puts the model, with "Choose..." to pick a game's assets folder.
  - Tick "Export after every change" and a Duck Forge game that is
    running shows each change at once.
  - Duck Forge Studio opens a game's models in tabs, and each tab is
    this Studio.


MODELS THAT MOVE  (new in 0.5)
  A recipe can say how a model moves: how far each part may turn, its
  poses, and animations made of poses. Open the robot, the door, the
  cart or the gnome from the start page to see.
  - In the picture's top right corner, "Play" plays an animation; pick
    which one beside it. Space plays and stops too.
  - Press O (or click "Pose") to pose the model. Pick a pose in the
    list on the right, click a part, and drag one of its coloured
    rings to turn it: it stops at the part's limits. Every drag is
    written to the recipe. "Mirror" makes the left-right copy of a
    pose; the part's limits are in the panel, to change.
  - In Pose mode the timeline runs along the bottom: the animation's
    keys are diamonds. Drag one to change its time, right-click it to
    remove it, drag along the ruler to move through time. "+ Key"
    adds the pose chosen at the time shown. Faint blue and orange
    outlines are the poses before and after.
  - Under the list of parts, "Animations" lists ready-made ones to
    copy into the model: idle, walk, wave and jump for a character
    whose parts are named body, head, left_arm, right_arm, left_leg
    and right_leg; open for a part named door; roll for four wheels.
    "Save" puts one of your model's animations in the folder
    "animations" beside it, for your other models to take.

  On the command line, to see the robot's walk as a strip of pictures:

    duck-mold render examples\robot.toml --animation walk --view right

  It writes examples\robot.walk.strip.png. To check every frame of it:

    duck-mold inspect examples\robot.toml --animation walk

  "Export" writes the animations into the .glb, and a Duck Forge game
  plays them by name: robot.play("walk").


FOR GAMES: WHAT WAS HIT, AND FOOTSTEPS  (new in 0.6)
  - Hit shapes: "Hits", at the top, outlines a simple box, ball or
    capsule round each part, in cyan. A game tests them to know which
    part a click or a bullet found. Select a part to choose its own
    shape in the panel on the right (or "none" for a part too small to
    aim at); with nothing selected, the panel says what the whole model
    has. New models start with a shape for every part.
  - Events: in Pose mode, right-click a key on the timeline, type a
    name (footstep) and click "Add event". A small cyan flag shows it.
    A game hears it each time the animation passes that key: a step
    sound, a puff of dust. The robot's walk has footsteps already.
  - "Fit to ground", beside Loop and Smooth, stands every pose of the
    animation on the ground, so a walk copied onto shorter legs
    doesn't sink into it.
  - Right-click a key: "Make this key's pose its own" gives it a copy
    of a pose other keys share, so a drag changes only it.
  - The timeline zooms: Ctrl and the mouse wheel, or the - and +
    buttons; "Fit" shows it all again.

  On the command line:

    duck-mold render examples\robot.toml --colliders
    duck-mold inspect examples\robot.toml --animation walk
    duck-mold edit examples\gnome.toml fit-ground walk


NEW IN 0.7
  - Nothing is lost: every change is written safely, Undo carries on
    after you close the Studio, and a recipe deleted while open can be
    saved again.
  - A tidier Studio: one toolbar, a status bar at the bottom, an axis
    gizmo, and View > Interface size to make everything bigger.
  - Far-away versions: View > Far version shows the simpler model a game
    draws when it is far off. The Model section on the right sets them.
  - With Hits on, a click picks what a game's click would.

  On the command line:

    duck-mold render examples\robot.toml --far 2
    duck-mold edit examples\robot.toml --batch my-edits.txt


YOUR MODELS ARE TEXT FILES
  A model is a recipe: a .toml file that lists its parts and where they
  join. The Studio changes that file every time you change something,
  so there is nothing to save, and the file is the model. You can open
  it in Notepad, and an AI agent can read and change it too, while the
  Studio is open: the Studio shows the change at once.


WHAT'S IN THIS FOLDER
  DuckMoldStudio.exe   the Studio: build models with the mouse
  duck-mold.exe        Duck Mold on the command line, for AI agents and
                       scripts: inspect, export, render and edit
                       recipes without a window. "duck-mold guide"
                       prints all an agent needs to know.
  examples\            recipes to open, look at and change
  docs\                the recipe format, and the guide for AI agents


A NEW VERSION
  The easiest way: DuckMoldSetup-<version>.exe from the Releases page
  installs Duck Mold for you (no administrator needed), in the Start
  menu, and installing a newer one replaces it. Or download the new zip,
  unzip it, and replace this folder with it. Keep your own models
  somewhere else (the Studio puts them in Documents\Duck Mold), never in
  this folder, so they are safe. Run "Create desktop shortcut.bat" again
  if you moved the folder.
